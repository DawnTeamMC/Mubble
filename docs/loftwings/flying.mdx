## The belt

A [loftwing]($loftwings/overview) carries nobody until it is wearing a **loftwing belt**. Use one on your bird to fit it, and use it again with an empty hand to climb on; dispensers can fit it too. Crouch and use instead to sit the bird down.

| Recipe | |
|---|---|
| Loftwing belt | Four leads in a ring |

## Taking off and steering

On the ground the loftwing walks wherever you steer it. Press **Jump** and it beats its wings and goes straight up: that is how it leaves the ground, and the same key is how it gains height for the rest of the flight.

In the air it never stops going forward. You do not steer it directly: it comes round to wherever you are looking, in its own time, and the faster it is going the wider the turn. You cannot get off in mid-air — that is what the belt is for.

**Sprint** to dash: a short burst of speed, after which the bird needs a moment before it can do it again.

## Speed is height

Point the nose down and the loftwing trades height for speed; point it up and it spends that speed back on height. The air takes its cut either way, so none of it is free, but it means a long dive is the fastest way to a high climb — and that pulling up out of a slow glide gets you nowhere at all.

## Wingbeats

Every beat of the wings costs a slice of a reserve, and that reserve is the only limit on how high a loftwing goes: hold **Jump** and it keeps climbing for as long as the reserve lasts, which is about enough to carry you from the sea to the clouds. Let go and gliding pays it back, at half the rate it is spent, and so does standing still on the ground.

A loftwing with an empty reserve neither stalls nor drops its own nose. It simply cannot beat its wings or dash any more, and the nose stays exactly where you point it.

## Landing

Stop beating the wings and the bird loses height on its own, whatever its nose is doing. Aim it at where you want to come down, let it glide in, and it is back on its feet the moment it touches the ground.

## Crashing

Fly into a wall at speed and the bird stops dead: you are thrown off and both of you are hurt. How much the loftwing takes depends on what it is wearing.

## Helmets

A loftwing helmet never wears out. It softens a crash and caps how fast the bird can swing its nose or its body round, and the heavier it is the more of both. Every helmet past a bare head slows how quickly it can raise or drop the nose, which is what a dive and a climb are made of; on the turn itself the cap only bites on the heaviest few, since a loftwing banks in its own slow time whatever it is wearing.

| Helmet | Crash soaked up | Fastest 90° swing |
|---|---|---|
| None | nothing | 0.4s |
| Leather | 1 heart | 1s |
| Golden | 2 hearts | 1.5s |
| Chainmail | 3 hearts | 2s |
| Iron | 4 hearts | 3s |
| Diamond | 6 hearts | 4s |
| Netherite | all of it | 5s |

Every helmet except the chainmail one is crafted the same way: eight of a material around a redstone lamp, with the bottom middle left empty. The netherite one is upgraded from the diamond one at a smithing table. The chainmail one is a [gift]($loftwings/bond#being-refused).

## Calling your loftwing

Your loftwing teaches you its call when it [picks you]($loftwings/bond), and hands you a **loftwing whistle**. Blow it and the bird comes, from wherever it is, as long as the chunks it is in are loaded and nothing is holding it. If you happen to be falling when you blow it, it slides in underneath you and you land on its back.

Lose the whistle and you can make another one from a feather and a copper ingot.
